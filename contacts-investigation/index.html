Contacts & list investigation — consolidated findings

Synthesized from the April 2026 investigation pack (Reforge + Snowflake). Internal working summary for the contacts prototype program.

At a glance

Import File upload is the highest-friction bulk path: roughly half of starters see errors; more than a quarter abandon after errors. Copy/paste is far calmer (~10% error rate) but sees heavy mid-funnel drop-off.

Activation Snowflake retention (March 2026, first six lifecycle months): users without a recorded contact upload show 0% activation vs. 59.5% for uploaders. Sending campaigns shows the same pattern (1.4% vs. 78.1%).

Lists Qualitative + behavioral data align on list/segment confusion, weak discoverability for “create list,” and high demand to see contacts within a specific list (e.g. “View contact lists”).

Positioning Initiative docs reframe work from “experiment?” to “reliability / Customer Happiness mandate” with a Q2 scoping posture and ROI narrative (~$5–8M/year addressable activation value in briefs; engineering cost still needs firm sizing).

Why contact import is not a side quest

Retention-model cuts in the pack show contact upload is effectively gating core product use—not an optional onboarding flourish.

Metric (first 6 months) With contact upload Without contact upload
Activation rate59.5%0.0%
Sent ≥1 campaign78.1%1.4%
Churn rate7.2%7.8%

Implication for this prototype: flows that help users add contacts (upload, paste, single, sync) should be treated as primary surfaces—clear states, honest validation, and recoverable errors matter as much as campaign builders.

File upload: experience & severity

Journey funnel (≈30 days, “Upload from file” starters)

  • 4,431 users clicked “Upload from file”; 99.8% reached the import screen.
  • 3,959 reached the final Import action; 548 explicitly cancelled; 472 silently abandoned (no Import, no Cancel).
  • Of those who clicked Import, 1,137 (~28.7%) hit a final import error (often permission/consent/surprise skips).

Mutually exclusive outcome mix (upload cohort analysis)

OutcomeShareNotes
Smooth success (no errors)49.7%Median ~4 minutes — target experience.
Success despite errors14.2%Often 2–3 repeats of the same error; tail users see many errors over hours or days.
Abandon after errors27.1%Blocked from finishing; strong “trying other software” language in qual.
Abandon, no tracked errors9.1%Confusion, distraction, or untracked issues.

Time & desperation signals

  • 50.1% of uploaders spread work across multiple days (~15 day average) to finish or give up.
  • 6.5% spent 30–120 minutes in a single session (median ~54 minutes for that bucket).
  • ~27% tried multiple file types (CSV ↔ Excel) — usually a header/structure issue, not format.

Root causes called out in the pack

  • Brittle header detection — reasonable labels (“E-mail”, “Email1”, “organization”) fail against rigid expectations.
  • System rejects valid files — thousands of errors on bona fide CSV/XLSX; the official template is cited as failing for a non-trivial number of users.
  • Stage-level failures — upload (format, JSON, timeouts), mapping (10,579 users in one cut), and late permission/consent surprises erode trust.
Representative frustration: repeated uploads, support calls, and explicit churn to competitors when imports still fail.

Add-contact methods (volume vs. pain)

From add-contacts traffic (~21K users / 30 days in the brief):

MethodApprox. share of visitorsError story
Single contact form~35%High usage; error telemetry called out as incomplete.
Copy / paste multiple~30%~9.8% error rate among those reaching Import — much lower than file upload, but less than half reach the final button.
File upload~21%~52.8% hit errors among upload starters — worst reliability, critical for bulk migrations.
Integrations~7%Error tracking gaps noted.

Design takeaway: prioritize upload reliability and “explain what will happen” before final Import; keep paste/single paths short and legible as fallbacks.

Lists, segments, and navigation

  • Import failures — highest convergent qual + quant signal; especially painful for active senders (~27% error rate in one sender-focused cut vs. ~22% for non-senders).
  • List vs. segment confusion — recurring qual theme; only ~16% of “create list” choices pick segments vs. static lists.
  • Navigation churn — large volumes of cross-clicks among Contacts, Lists, Segments, and Audience sidebars suggest users hunting for the right home.
  • “View contact lists” — tens of thousands of users / hundreds of thousands of clicks — people need to see membership and manage lists from a contact-centric view.
  • List creation dialog — ~38K opens vs. ~30.5K static selections and ~5.9K segment selections; ~7K gap interpreted as hesitation or confusion at type choice.
  • Operational work — export, bulk delete, merge, and duplicate cleanup show up frequently in feedback and events (ongoing hygiene, not one-off).

Initiative framing (from CONTACT_IMPORT_FIX_INITIATIVE)

  • Stated mandate: treat broken import as critical workflow debt, not an A/B curiosity.
  • Customer promise narrative: move a large monthly cohort from multi-day / multi-error loops to sub–10 minute success with preview, clearer mapping, and fewer surprise dead-ends.
  • Business narrative: combine activation lift + support deflection; briefs cite order-of-week payback if engineering lands in the low single-digit month range (needs validation).
  • Execution ask: structured eng scoping, opportunity-cost stack rank vs. other activation blockers, optional competitive benchmarking on import success rates.

Open questions the pack still flags

  • Opportunity cost — is import the highest-leverage activation bet vs. other blockers?
  • Engineering scope — firm estimates by workstream (headers, parsing, permissions UX, preflight, template fixes).
  • Unit economics — precise support ticket cost and deflection model.
  • Competitive bar — do peers hit 80–90% smooth import or share the same pain?
  • Segmentation — error and abandonment rates by tier, industry, and tenure (where available).
