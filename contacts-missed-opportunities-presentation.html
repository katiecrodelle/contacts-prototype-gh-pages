Why this exists

The initiative is focused where it should be — and this brief widens the lens.

The contacts initiative has correctly identified that import is broken and that fixing it is a business-critical mandate. The activation data is unambiguous: users without a recorded contact upload have a 0% activation rate vs. 59.5% for uploaders. Fixing the broken import flow is the right Q2 call.

But the data contains signals that point well beyond “fix what’s broken.” This brief documents those signals — the opportunities hiding in the same dataset that aren’t currently part of the conversation.

The activation data has a second story

Nobody is telling this one loudly enough yet.

Metric (first 6 months) With contact upload Without contact upload
Activation rate59.5%0.0%
Sent ≥1 campaign78.1%1.4%
Churn rate7.2%7.8%

The churn gap between users who upload and users who don’t is only 0.6 percentage points. People who successfully get contacts in are still churning at a meaningful rate. Import gates entry — but something else fails to hold users once they’re in.

Fixing import gets users to the starting line. It doesn’t make them runners.

What does the product feel like for someone who imported 500 contacts but doesn’t know what to do next?

Structural blind spots

Three patterns the initiative conversation underweights.

1 Copy/paste is a stealth winner

~30% of add-contact traffic; ~10% error rate vs. ~53% for file upload — already far more reliable. Yet less than half who start copy/paste reach the final Import button: mid-funnel UX drop-off, not a technical ceiling. One of the most investable quick wins — and almost absent from the initiative narrative because it isn’t “broken enough.”

What happens between “user pastes content” and “user clicks Import” that loses more than half of them?

2 Single contact form — highest volume, worst telemetry

~35% of traffic — more than any other path — with error telemetry flagged as incomplete. We may be blind to serious friction on the most-used surface.

What does single-contact add feel like for a non-technical user who just got a business card at a networking event?

3 Segmentation is the 84% problem

Only ~16% of list creation picks segments vs. static lists. Beyond navigation, users who only use static lists get a Rolodex, not a marketing engine — weaker targeting, signals, and perceived ROI. A retention problem in disguise.

Is low segment adoption discoverability, comprehension, or trust? (Each needs a different design response.)

Opportunities outside “fix import”

Where differentiation and retention can compound after the floor is solid.

4 Contact acquisition

We optimize for contacts users already have. Growth also lives where SMBs earn new contacts:

  • Signup forms & landing pages
  • QR codes — events, retail, print
  • Text-to-join
  • Mobile capture — card scanner, address book

Category peers have invested in capture-at-the-moment; that story is largely absent from our initiative framing.

5 Integrations — import can become optional

~7% of traffic; error tracking gaps. The path to a self-maintaining database for Shopify, Google, Eventbrite, Square, POS stacks. Competitive analysis: differentiation increasingly comes from integrations — yet this path is underexplored internally.

6 The migration cohort

~27% of uploaders try CSV and Excel — classic migrator behavior, high intent, high frustration, churn risk when imports fail. A migration-specific experience could ship empathy with a fraction of general-parser scope.

7 Data hygiene as engagement

Merge, duplicates, cleanup show up in qual and events — framed as one-off maintenance. Proactive hygiene (“47 duplicates — merge?”) re-engages users, signals intelligence, and supports deliverability.

What this means for design

Two tracks — fix the floor, build the ceiling.

Q2 is solving the right problem. These signals mean design should hold parallel truths: reliability is mandatory — and it is not sufficient for retention on its own.

Track 1 — Fix the floor

Reliable, predictable, recoverable import. Improve copy/paste completion. Instrument the single-contact path so we can see reality.

Track 2 — Build the ceiling

“What do users do once contacts are in?” Segmentation onboarding, hygiene surfaces, mobile capture moments, acquisition tools — staged so they don’t dilute Q2.
