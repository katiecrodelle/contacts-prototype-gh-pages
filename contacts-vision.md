# Contacts vision story

**Katie DeVoe** · Lead Product Designer · Constant Contact · April 2026

*Grounded in the April 2026 Reforge + Snowflake investigation pack, competitive landscape notes, the internal “missed opportunities” synthesis on contacts & list growth, and lifecycle + success customer cohorts (internal, March 2026). Contacts initiative with **Customer Happiness** (Liz / Oz and partners).*

---

## Executive summary (for leadership)

Constant Contact’s activation data is blunt: without getting contacts into the product, customers do not activate. Fixing broken import is the right Q2 mandate—it is the floor we have to get right.

The retention story is the sharper surprise in the same data: **people who got contacts in and people who never did churn at nearly the same rate.** Upload is not a loyalty machine. It clears a hurdle. What happens next—whether the product helps them **trust the list**, **organize it**, **keep it healthy**, and **feel smart about who to reach**—is where we win or lose them. That is the work I want us to be famous for in Contacts: not only fewer errors at upload, but a **sticky arc after the file lands**.

Our vision is to treat **Contacts as the audience engine**—not a static warehouse. We will differentiate by pairing **import people can trust** with **ongoing value**: paste and manual entry that feel obvious, integrations that keep records current where stacks allow, migration-native guidance for switchers, capture surfaces for real-world list growth, and proactive list health that signals intelligence instead of hiding maintenance in settings.

We execute in two parallel tracks: **fix the floor** (reliability, telemetry, mid-funnel completion) and **build the ceiling** (what customers do with contacts once they are in). That sequencing protects Q2 while keeping the north star honest: **retention lives after the gate.**

---

## Where we focus in the lifecycle

Lifecycle cohorts (onboarding, growing, steady, declining, resting) describe how different slices of the base show up over time. **In this Customer Happiness core work we primarily design for Growing, Steady, and Declining customers**—people who already have or are building a real audience and need the product to stay useful as volume, habits, and doubt shift. **Onboarding is largely owned elsewhere;** we do not pretend this initiative is the full first-run story, and scope can evolve as priorities change. Resting customers need a light touch (do no harm, preserve trust); the emotional center of this vision is **helping people who already crossed the contact threshold find lasting value**.

## Why I am writing this

The organization has correctly centered the contacts initiative on import reliability. I am fully aligned—that work is business-critical.

As a designer, my job is also to hold the product’s *whole* story. The investigation pack and missed-opportunities synthesis surface signals that are easy to lose when we are rightly heads-down on errors and headers: copy/paste completion, single-entry blind spots, segmentation adoption, integrations as a self-maintaining path, migration empathy, and hygiene as engagement. I pressure-test those signals against **Growing, Steady, and Declining** jobs to be done—not only day zero. This document is my stake in the ground for how we talk about Contacts beyond “fix what is broken.”

---

## The honest read on the data

Activation hinges on contact upload. No upload, essentially no activation. That is the headline, and it should stay the headline.

The line that should change how we invest is quieter: **the gap in churn between “got contacts in” and “never did” is small.** Success at import does not buy much loyalty on its own. That means **import is a gate, not the journey.** If we stop when the CSV parses, we leave most of the retention problem on the table. The product has to earn the next months: **clarity after import**, **confidence to send**, **lists and segments that feel legible**, **hygiene that feels like help instead of homework**, and **paths that match how SMBs actually work**—not another week lost to spreadsheets and support threads.

**Design question I want us to keep visible:** What does Constant Contact feel like the week after someone imports 200 names—especially if they are not sure what to send, who to segment, or whether their list is “good enough”?

---

## Vision: Contacts as the audience engine

I want Contacts to be the place where small businesses **trust** their audience data—because it is accurate, reachable, understandable, and ready for action.

That means:

- **Getting people in** should feel calm and recoverable—whether someone brings a spreadsheet, pastes a short list, adds one person from a business card, or connects a system that should stay in sync.
- **Understanding the audience** should not require a marketing degree. Lists and segments should read as outcomes (“who I am trying to reach”) rather than internal objects users have to decode.
- **Keeping the audience healthy** should feel like the product is on their side—surfacing duplicates, gaps, and permission risks before they become deliverability or reputation problems.

Contacts is not a filing cabinet. It is the **engine** that makes the rest of the product credible.

---

## Retention: what we are actually solving for

Retention, for this initiative, is not “more rows in a table.” It is **confidence + momentum**:

1. **Confidence** — “I believe this list is right, legal to mail, and representative of my customers.”
2. **Momentum** — “I know what to do next in the product, and I can do it without another multi-day detour.”

Import reliability builds confidence at entry. The ceiling work builds momentum after entry: segmentation that feels worth learning, hygiene nudges that feel helpful, and flows that answer “now what?” without sending people back to a spreadsheet.

**Why that matters when churn is almost flat across the upload divide:** every improvement that only optimizes the gate competes for a **thin slice** of differentiated retention. Every improvement that helps someone **use, organize, and protect** their audience after the gate competes for the **whole rest of the relationship**. That is the bet this vision is written to support.

---

## Differentiation (how we win against the category)

Competitors are not standing still: they invest in **capture moments** (mobile, events, QR), **deep CRM-style depth** on some fronts, and **integration ecosystems** that reduce manual import altogether. Constant Contact wins when we are the best partner for the SMB who is juggling fifteen tools and cannot afford a week on data cleanup.

**Where we should lead:**

| Pillar | What customers should feel | How it shows up in the work |
|--------|-----------------------------|------------------------------|
| **Trustworthy entry** | “The product met me where I was.” | Upload reliability, honest validation, migration-native guidance for switchers, fewer surprise dead-ends at Import. |
| **Low-friction paths** | “I did not need a CSV for this.” | Copy/paste and manual entry that are short, legible, and measured end-to-end—not only error rates, but completion. |
| **Self-maintaining data** | “It stays current without heroic effort.” | Integrations and sync paths that are discoverable, dependable, and well-instrumented—so we can improve what we cannot yet see. |
| **Audience intelligence** | “It helps me send smarter, not just more.” | Segmentation framed as value, not jargon; proactive hygiene; clear signals when a list is not ready to mail. |
| **Real-world growth** | “I can grow the list where life happens.” | Acquisition surfaces over time—forms, QR, text-to-join, mobile capture—staged so they strengthen retention without distracting from the Q2 floor. |

I am intentionally not turning this into a competitor scorecard in writing. The differentiation story is **outcomes for SMBs**, not feature parity tables—legal and comms can help us name peers when we publish externally.

---

## Closing

I am proud that we are treating import as customer happiness work, not as a side project. My ask is that we hold the full picture at the same time: **fix the floor without mistaking it for the relationship.** The prize is customers who **stay** because Contacts helped them **keep finding value** after the list was already in the room.

— Katie
